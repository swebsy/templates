How a family case works, start to finish.

Most family cases take eight to fourteen months. This is the whole road: what happens, what we do at each turn, what we need from you, and what no honest lawyer can promise.

Five steps, one lawyer the whole way.

The same lawyer reads your file at every step, so you never explain your family twice.

  1. 1

    The consultation

    Thirty minutes, in person or by video. We tell you which forms apply, what it costs and how long it usually takes. If you don't need a lawyer, we say so.

  2. 2

    We build the file

    You get a checklist in your language, and we collect the documents with you. Most families are ready to file in three to five weeks.

  3. 3

    We file, and USCIS confirms

    A complete petition, read twice before it goes. The receipt notice arrives two to four weeks later, and from then on we watch the case for you.

  4. 4

    Fingerprints and the interview

    Fingerprints usually come within two months. The interview is often eight to fourteen months in. We run a mock interview with you first, and a lawyer sits beside you on the day.

  5. 5

    Approved

    The card comes by mail. We check every line on it, and tell you when to renew or when you can apply for citizenship.

What to bring to the first meeting.

Bring what you have. Missing papers are normal, and finding them is part of our job.

  • Passports, and any visas or I-94 records
  • Birth certificates for everyone named in the case
  • Your marriage certificate, and any divorce decrees
  • Every letter you have had from USCIS or a consulate
  • Your latest tax return and recent pay stubs
  • Any arrest or court records, even old or dismissed ones

What we need from you.

  • Answers within a week when we ask a question.
  • Word of any change: a new address, a new job, a trip abroad, a new child.
  • The whole story, including the hard parts. A problem we know about is one we can plan for.

What we can't promise.

  • An approval. The decision belongs to USCIS, and anyone who guarantees one is not being straight with you.
  • A date. We give you typical ranges, and we tell you when a case is running slow.
  • That USCIS won't ask for more. Many cases get a request for evidence; our fee covers the answer.
