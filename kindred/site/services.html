Six kinds of family case, one flat fee each.

Find yours by what is happening in your family. Our fee is agreed in writing before we start. Government fees are paid straight to USCIS, and we list those in writing too.

Not sure which? Ask us

Form I-130 · I-485

Marrying a US citizen or green-card holder

A marriage green card, whether your spouse is already here or waiting abroad. We prepare the petition, the proof of your life together and the interview.

From $3,450, flat

Who qualifies

Spouses of US citizens and of green-card holders. Same-sex marriages count the same.

Typical timeline

Ten to fourteen months from inside the US. Twelve to eighteen through a consulate abroad.

What our fee covers

The petition and green-card application, the evidence binder, work and travel permits while you wait, and a mock interview.

Form I-129F

Engaged to someone abroad

The K-1 fiancé visa brings your partner here to marry within 90 days. After the wedding we file their green card, so one firm handles both steps.

From $2,900, flat

Who qualifies

US citizens engaged to someone abroad, both free to marry, who have met in person in the last two years.

Typical timeline

Twelve to sixteen months to the visa, then the green-card application after you marry.

What our fee covers

The petition, consular interview preparation in your partner's language, and the green-card filing after the wedding.

Form I-130

Bringing your parents, children or siblings

Who can petition for whom depends on whether you are a citizen or a green-card holder, and the wait depends on the category. We tell you the real wait before you pay anything.

From $2,600, flat

Who qualifies

Citizens can petition for parents, children of any age and siblings. Green-card holders can petition for a spouse and unmarried children.

Typical timeline

About a year for the parents of a citizen. Years for siblings and married children; we read the current visa bulletin with you.

What our fee covers

The petition, the affidavit of support, consular processing and the interview packet.

Form I-751

Your two-year green card is running out

If you married less than two years before your green card, it expires after two years. File in the 90 days before it does, together or, if the marriage has ended, on your own.

From $1,900, flat

Who qualifies

Conditional residents through marriage, including people who are divorced, widowed or leaving an abusive marriage.

Typical timeline

Your card is extended by 48 months the day we file. A decision usually takes one to two years.

What our fee covers

The petition, the evidence of your shared life, the waiver argument if you file alone, and the interview if one is called.

Ask about your case

Form I-601A

A past overstay is in the way

Time spent here without status can bar a green card for three or ten years. A provisional waiver asks USCIS to forgive it before you leave for your consular interview, so the family is apart for weeks rather than years.

From $4,200, flat

Who qualifies

Spouses, sons and daughters of US citizens or green-card holders, when their absence would cause that relative extreme hardship.

Typical timeline

Often two and a half to three and a half years for the waiver, then the interview abroad.

What our fee covers

The hardship case: medical, financial and family evidence, expert letters and a written legal argument.

Ask about your case

Form N-400

Becoming a US citizen

Naturalization, the civics test and the interview. Most green-card holders can apply after five years, or three if married to a citizen.

From $1,450, flat

Who qualifies

Green-card holders with five years of residence, or three if married to a US citizen. We check your record first.

Typical timeline

Six to ten months from filing to the oath ceremony in Sacramento.

What our fee covers

The application, a record check, civics and English practice, and a lawyer beside you at the interview.
