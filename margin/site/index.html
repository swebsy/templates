The key

Most lost marks are method marks, not missing knowledge.

I marked GCSE maths for an exam board for six summers. Examiners give marks for steps, not just answers. Here's the key, so you can read your child's papers the way I do.

M1
Method mark. For showing the right idea, even if the arithmetic goes wrong later.
5x − 3x = 12 + 7
A1
Accuracy mark. Only given if the method marks before it were earned. One slip can cost two marks.
x = 9.5
B1
Independent mark. For a correct fact or value on its own, like a unit or a labelled axis.
v = 12 m/s

Ask

Questions parents ask me.

What if my child doesn't want to come?

Most don't, at first. The trial is mostly talking and marking a paper they've already done, so there's nothing new to fail. If it isn't right for them, we stop there.

Do you follow my child's exam board?

Yes. Tell me the board and I'll use its past papers and mark schemes from the first lesson.

How will I know it's working?

Every six weeks your child sits a timed paper with me and you get the marks, question by question, with one line on what changed.

Can I sit in?

Of course, for any lesson. A parent is always home for students under 16, and online lessons can be recorded if you'd like.

What if we need to stop?

Just tell me. Single lessons have no notice period, and unused lessons in a block are refunded.
