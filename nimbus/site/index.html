Four sensors, and what they actually tell you

Ranges and tolerances below are the figures we test to, not the best case from a supplier datasheet.

ParticulatesPM2.5 / PM10 · laser scattering0–500 µg/m³ · ±10%

Frying, toasting, candles, a wood burner, pollen and road dust. PM2.5 is the fraction small enough to pass the lungs and enter the blood, which is why it is the number most air-quality guidance is written around.

Carbon dioxideNDIR, dual channel400–5000 ppm · ±50 ppm ±3%

A proxy for how much of the air in the room you have already breathed. Outdoors sits near 420 ppm. A closed bedroom with two people in it reaches 2,000 ppm by morning, and most people report feeling dull somewhere above 1,000.

Volatile organicsMOx, temperature compensatedindex 0–500

Solvents off cleaning products, fresh paint, new furniture and printer toner. Reported as a relative index rather than a concentration, because a low-cost sensor cannot honestly tell you which compound it found.

Temperature and humiditydigital, on a thermally isolated arm−10 to 50 °C ± 0.3 °C · 0–95 %RH ± 2%

Below 40% relative humidity you get static, dust and dry throats. Above 60% for any length of time you get mould in the cold corners. The sensor sits away from the board so its own heat does not skew the reading.

Works with what you already run

Nimbus One is a sensor, so it reports into whichever system you already use rather than asking for another app on the home screen.

  • Apple Home

    Appears as four HomeKit sensors. Automations evaluate on your own hub, so nothing leaves the house.

  • Matter

    Certified as a Matter 1.3 air-quality sensor. Pairs with any Matter controller using the code on the base.

  • Google Home

    Ask a speaker for the current reading, or start a routine when a threshold is crossed.

  • Amazon Alexa

    Announcements on an Echo — useful for carbon dioxide, which you cannot smell or feel coming.

  • Home Assistant

    Local polling over the LAN, discovered automatically. No cloud account, no API key, no rate limit.

  • Anything that reads JSON

    An unauthenticated read-only endpoint on the local network, documented on the support page. Turn it off in settings if you would rather it were not there.

It sits on a shelf and gets on with it. No screen to wake, no app to open, no subscription to forget to cancel.
