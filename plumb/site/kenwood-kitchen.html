What they asked for

Marcus and Lee cook together most nights. They wanted the galley opened to the dining room, more counter than they’d ever had, and a kitchen that still looked like it belonged in a 1924 house.

What we found behind the walls

Knob‑and‑tube wiring still live in two circuits, and a joist under the old sink soft from a slow leak. Neither was in the first estimate, because nobody could see them.

We stopped, showed them both, and priced the fix the same afternoon: $4,100. They said yes, and the schedule held.

Week 4: the ceiling open to the joists, old wiring traced back to the panel

How the 11 weeks went

We kept one room of the house working and the rest of it clean.

Out with the old

In the first two weeks we put up dust walls, took the old kitchen out and opened the dining-room wall onto a new beam.

Inside the walls

Weeks three to five went on rewiring, the joist repair and new plumbing. Every inspection passed the first time.

Kitchen in

From week six, plaster, cabinets and counters, then tile all the way to the ceiling by week nine.

Handover

The last two weeks: floor, paint and appliances, then a walkthrough and a folder with every warranty and paint code.

“We cooked on a hot plate in the dining room for eleven weeks, and never once found dust in the bedrooms.”

Marcus and Lee, Kenwood
