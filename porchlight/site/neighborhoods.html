Six neighborhoods, from the porch

What it’s like to live there, what houses cost, and where we’d get coffee. Each one written by whichever of us knows it best.

College Hill

Victorians · $650k–$1.6M

Every other house has a plaque with the year it was built, and the university keeps the sidewalks busy all year. Parking is the only complaint.

Written by Claire, who has lived on Angell Street since 2009.

Near: Wayland Square shops · the Athenaeum library · Prospect Terrace Park

Fox Point

Cottages · $520k–$900k

Sailors’ cottages two blocks from the river, and the best walk to work in the city. Small houses, small yards, big porches.

Written by Mei, who walks through it to our office every morning.

Near: Wickenden Street · India Point Park · the riverwalk

Armory

Triple‑deckers · $480k–$800k

The neighborhood where you buy a triple‑decker, live on one floor and let the other two help pay for it. Murals, a Thursday farmers’ market, a park in the middle.

Written by Sam, who lives a block from the Armory.

Near: Dexter Training Ground · Broadway restaurants · the Armory market

Summit

Shingle and colonial · $550k–$950k

Quiet streets of shingled houses with real yards, a short walk to Hope Street. Families stay; houses come up maybe a dozen times a year.

Written by Claire, who sold her first house here.

Near: Hope Street shops · Summit Avenue park · the bike path

Elmhurst

Colonials · $420k–$700k

Colonials and long porches on wide, tree‑lined streets near the college. The most first‑time buyers we work with, and Mei’s own street.

Written by Mei, who lives here.

Near: Pleasant Valley Parkway · Smith Street cafés · Mount Pleasant library

Edgewood

Bungalows · $380k–$650k

Bungalows by the bay, just over the Cranston line. More house for the money, a garden big enough for tomatoes, and a short ride to downtown.

Written by Mei, who bought her own first house here.

Near: Stillhouse Cove · Broad Street shops · Roger Williams Park
